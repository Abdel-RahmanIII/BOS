# 2.2 Go-to-market and marketing plan

## Positioning and first shop segment

> For owner-operators of busy, multi-chair men’s barbershops in New Cairo and Nasr City who need to manage peak-hour walk-ins fairly, BarberQueue Egypt is a queue and front-desk system that keeps the walk-in order visible and gives customers a live wait for their preferred barber; unlike the usual combination of a paper register and phone or WhatsApp updates, we use a single queue for the shop’s service log and the wait times customers check in their mobile browser before leaving home.

Start in New Cairo and Nasr City with upper-mid-market shops that have **3–8 chairs**, charge **100–250 EGP per haircut**, see substantial evening or weekend walk-in traffic and use paper logs. Qualify for access to the owner and a named front-desk lead who can run the trial on a phone or tablet. The owner buys, the front desk keeps the queue current, and customers who regularly request a specific barber have the clearest reason to check it. Milestone 1’s six interviews covered two shops, both of which declined reservations to protect first-come, first-served service. Lead with per-barber waits and fair walk-in handling. [Milestone 1 founding memo, §§2–3 and §8](../../MIlestone%201/Milestone1.md)

## Core messages

| Audience | Message |
| --- | --- |
| Shop owner | Give regulars a live wait, keep walk-in order visible and see which services each chair completed. |
| Front desk and barbers | Record walk-ins and close completed services in one queue so customers can check availability themselves. |

## Sales-led growth motion

Founder visits are the primary acquisition channel. The owner has to agree to change the front-desk workflow, and staff have to update the queue during busy shifts for customers to trust the wait. Ahmed qualifies the shop, demonstrates the workflow to the owner and front-desk lead, sets up the shop, trains staff, observes a peak shift and reviews the trial with the owner. Nooreldeen joins early demos and reviews to capture objections and guide product decisions. Shop QR codes and direct links help customers adopt the product once staff are using it.

The price hypothesis is **1,000 EGP per shop per month after a free 30-day trial**, or **12,000 EGP annualized** at the same rate. At this contract value, keep the sales process local and use a standard demo, onboarding checklist and trial review. One owner expressed interest in a free trial during discovery. Paid conversion is the commercial test. [Milestone 1 founding memo, §§3 and 9 and Deliverable 1.2](../../MIlestone%201/Milestone1.md)

Track **qualified shops → owner demos → trials started → active trials → paid shops**. A shop starts its trial when setup and staff training are complete. Count it as activated once the front desk logs walk-ins and closes services digitally on an observed peak shift and its customer wait link is live. Count it as paid after the first subscription payment. Measure days from qualification to payment, including the 30-day trial.

## Beachhead and Launch Sequencing

## Beachhead

**First segment:** multi-chair men's barbershops (3–8 chairs, EGP 100–250 per haircut) in **Nasr City and New Cairo** that run a first-come, first-served queue and log services on paper.

**Why this segment first:**
1. **Dense and reachable.** Up to 377 listed shops (340 in Nasr City, 37 in New Cairo) [1][2] sit in two neighbouring districts, so the founders can sell and onboard every shop in person.
2. **Little competition.**
   - Fresha lists only 8 barbers in all of Cairo Governorate [3].
   - Ehgezly's 9 barbershops are all in New Cairo (Milestone 1, Section 5).
   - So most shops in the beachhead have no booking tool, and Ehgezly's presence suggests New Cairo shops are open to booking apps.
3. **Winnable size.** Early adopters are about 16% of a market [4], which is about 60 of the 377 shops. Our Year-1 target of 24 shops fits within them.

**Calculation:** 340 + 37 = 377 shops, which is 12.4% of Cairo's 3,037 listed barbershops (Milestone 1, Section 4).

**Note:** 377 is an upper bound. The listings include all men's barbershops, not only 3–8 chair shops charging EGP 100–250. The private beta confirms what share fits the ICP.

## Channel Strategy

We listed every channel that could reach barbershop owners, then compared the three most promising on cost, reach and fit with our ICP. Channels are ranked first by whether they can win shops from day one with zero existing customers, then by expected CAC.

## Channels Compared

| Rank | Channel | Cost driver | Reach | Fit with ICP | Expected CAC | Months to recover CAC* | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Founder-led field sales**: visit shops, demo the queue live, set up on the spot on the shop's own device | Salary EGP 10,000/month + transport EGP 1,225/month | All 377 beachhead shops, reached on foot. About 4 signings a month per seller | **High.** The owner is in the shop, pays for no software today and needs in-person setup | **EGP 2,600** (EGP 600 cash-only in Year 1) | 2.6 | **Primary.** The only channel sure to work with zero customers |
| **2** | **Inside sales (WhatsApp/phone)**: contact shops from directory listings, send a short demo video, then book one setup visit when the owner is interested | Salary EGP 5,000/month + phone EGP 300/month + one setup trip per signed shop | About 330 shops contacted a month: the whole beachhead in about a month, and the rest of Cairo with no travel | **Medium.** Cheap and scalable, but cold messages to owners who have never paid for software may convert poorly | **EGP 850** | 0.9 | **Second.** Cheapest per shop if it converts. Tested alongside field sales in Year 1 and scaled from Year 2 |
| **3** | **Shop-to-shop referrals**: an active shop refers a nearby shop and earns one free month | One free month (EGP 1,000) to the referrer + one onboarding trip (EGP 45) | Limited to shops' own networks. Starts at zero and grows with active shops (24 by month 12) | **High.** Owners and barbers know nearby shops, and barbers move between shops | **EGP 1,050** | 1.1 | **Third.** Cheap and trusted, but starts only once we have satisfied paying shops |

\*Months to recover CAC = CAC ÷ EGP 1,000 monthly subscription (Milestone 1 price).

### How each CAC is calculated

**1. Founder-led field sales**
- Salary: we value a seller at the median Egyptian sales representative's base salary, EGP 120,000 a year, or EGP 10,000 a month [1].
- Transport: 550 km a month (25 km × 22 days) ÷ 10 km per litre = 55 litres × EGP 22.25 (92-octane [2]) = EGP 1,225.
- Signings: one shop a week, about 4.3 a month (the Milestone 1 SOM assumption).
- CAC = (10,000 + 1,225) ÷ 4.3 = **EGP 2,600**.
- In Year 1 the founders are unpaid, so the cash cost is transport only: about 1,225 ÷ 2 shops = **EGP 600**.

**2. Inside sales (WhatsApp/phone)**
- Salary: the median inside sales representative in Cairo earns EGP 60,000 a year, or EGP 5,000 a month [3].
- Contacts: 15 shops a day × 22 days = 330 shops a month.
- Signings: 2% of contacts sign, the low end of the 2–5% lead-to-customer range [4]: 330 × 2% = 6.6 shops a month.
- Monthly cost: 5,000 salary + 300 phone + setup trips (6.6 × EGP 45 ≈ 300) = EGP 5,600.
- CAC = 5,600 ÷ 6.6 = **EGP 850**.
- Risk: cold outreach can convert far below 2%; lead lists can close below 0.1% [5]. At 0.5%, CAC rises to about EGP 3,400, above field sales.

**3. Shop-to-shop referrals**
- CAC = EGP 1,000 free month + EGP 45 for one 20 km onboarding trip = **EGP 1,050**.
- Referrals convert leads to deals at 3.6%, the highest of any B2B channel [5].

## Assumptions

1. Field transport (25 km a day, 10 km per litre) and the one-shop-a-week signing rate are our assumptions. The signing rate matches Milestone 1.
2. Inside sales contacts (15 a day), the 2% sign-up rate and the EGP 300 phone cost are our assumptions. The 2% is a general B2B benchmark [4], not measured for Egyptian barbershops.
3. The free first month every shop gets is not counted in CAC. Referral CAC counts only the extra free month given to the referrer.

## Why These Three Channels

**Why these three were shortlisted.** Each reaches the shop owner directly, the person who signs and pays, and each is a different kind of channel:
- **Field sales:** in-person selling.
- **Inside sales:** remote selling.
- **Referrals:** word of mouth.

This shows the trade-off between certainty, cost and scale rather than three versions of the same approach.

**Considered and not taken:**
- **Meta ads (Facebook/Instagram):** expected CAC of about EGP 8,100, or 8.1 months to recover.
  - Egypt's Facebook CPM is $1.50–2.50 [6], with a 1.93% click-through rate [7].
  - At EGP 54.70 per USD [8], that is about EGP 5.7 per click.
  - Only about 0.07% of clicks become paying shops: 2% of clicks become leads [9] × 3.5% of leads become customers [4].
  - Shop owners can't be targeted as a group, so most of the budget reaches customers instead. Better suited to the customer side of the marketplace later.
- **Google search ads:** owners don't search for queue software, since they pay for none today.
- **Barber-supply distributors:** their reps already visit every shop, but they have no clear reason to sell our product. A referral fee is too small to motivate them, and pushing a third-party product risks the trust their own business depends on. Revisited only if a distributor shows interest.

**Why field sales ranks first:**
1. **It works from day one.** Referrals need satisfied shops first, and the inside sales conversion rate is untested on barbershop owners. Field sales is the only channel certain to sign the first shops.
2. **It fits a small deal.** Each shop pays EGP 12,000 a year (about $220). That is too little to justify long sales cycles or paid ads, but the product still needs a person to demo and set it up. Founders selling door to door in two dense districts keep each sale cheap. At EGP 2,600, the CAC is under 3 months of subscription.
3. **It fits the buyer.** The owner pays for no software today and runs the shop on paper (Milestone 1 interviews). He needs a live demo and in-person setup, which no message or ad can replace.
4. **It is already in our plan.** The Milestone 1 SOM plan assumes founders selling in Year 1 and sales reps in Years 2–3.

Inside sales runs alongside field sales in Year 1 to test its conversion rate. If it reaches 2%, it becomes the cheaper way to scale from Year 2, with one setup visit to close each sale. Referrals are switched on once the first shops are paying.

## 90-Day Launch Plan

The first 90 days start the private beta. We sign 3 pilot shops, convert them to paying, and add 4 more shops (2 a month, as in Milestone 1). Shops run the system in a browser on the shop's own device, so we buy no hardware.

## Sales Process

**Pipeline stages:**
1. **First contact:** visit or WhatsApp message. Check that the shop fits the ICP.
2. **Demo:** 15-minute live demo at the front desk.
3. **Free month:** set up on the shop's own device and train staff.
4. **Paid:** EGP 1,000 a month by InstaPay or mobile wallet.
5. **Referral:** ask for one referral after the first paid month.

**Expected sales cycle:**
- About 1 week from first contact to the free month, because the owner decides alone.
- About 5–6 weeks to the first payment.

**Who sells:**
- **Ahmed (COO)** leads field sales and onboarding.
- **Nooreldeen (CEO)** co-sells and runs the WhatsApp/phone outreach.
- A sales rep joins in Year 2 (Milestone 1 SOM plan).
