# 2.2 Go-to-market and marketing plan

## Positioning and first shop segment

For owner-operators of busy, multi-chair men’s barbershops in Greater Cairo who need to manage walk-ins fairly at peak hours, BarberQueue Egypt is a queue and front-desk system that gives clients a live wait for their preferred barber and gives staff a digital service log. It replaces the separate paper register and phone or WhatsApp availability updates with a single queue that customers can check in their mobile browser before leaving home.

Start in New Cairo and Nasr City with upper-mid-market shops that have **3–8 chairs**, charge **100–250 EGP per haircut**, see substantial evening or weekend walk-in traffic and use paper logs. Qualify for access to the owner and a named front-desk lead who can run the trial on a phone or tablet. The owner buys, the front desk keeps the queue current, and customers who regularly request a specific barber have the clearest reason to check it. Milestone 1’s six interviews covered two shops, both of which declined reservations to protect first-come, first-served service. Lead with per-barber waits and fair walk-in handling. [Milestone 1 founding memo, §§2–3 and §8](../../MIlestone%201/Milestone1.md)

## Core messages

| Audience | Message |
| --- | --- |
| Shop owner | Give regulars a live wait, keep walk-in order visible and see which services each chair completed. |
| Front desk and barbers | Record walk-ins and close completed services in one queue so customers can check availability themselves. |
| Customer | Open the shop’s link to check your barber’s live wait before you leave. Scan the shop’s QR code to save the link for your next visit. |

## Sales-led launch

Founder visits are the primary acquisition channel. The owner has to agree to change the front-desk workflow, and staff have to update the queue during busy shifts for customers to trust the wait. Ahmed qualifies the shop, demonstrates the workflow to the owner and front-desk lead, sets up the shop, trains staff, observes a peak shift and reviews the trial with the owner. Nooreldeen joins early demos and reviews to capture objections and guide product decisions. Shop QR codes and direct links help customers adopt the product once staff are using it.

The price hypothesis is **1,000 EGP per shop per month after a free 30-day trial**, or **12,000 EGP annualized** at the same rate. At this contract value, keep the sales process local and use a standard demo, onboarding checklist and trial review. One owner expressed interest in a free trial during discovery. Paid conversion is the commercial test. [Milestone 1 founding memo, §§3 and 9 and Deliverable 1.2](../../MIlestone%201/Milestone1.md)

Track **qualified shops → owner demos → trials started → active trials → paid shops**. A shop starts its trial when setup and staff training are complete. Count it as activated once the front desk logs walk-ins and closes services digitally on an observed peak shift and its customer wait link is live. Count it as paid after the first subscription payment. Measure days from qualification to payment, including the 30-day trial.

## Launch phases and exit criteria

| Phase | Goal | Exit criteria |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Private beta · months 1–3** | Prove that three shops in the first segment can keep a live per-barber queue accurate during busy shifts and convert after the free trial. Fix and retest the section 2.3 front-desk flow before staff rely on it. | Run three trials. In four weeks of peak-shift checks covering at least **100 visits** across the pilot, enter **≥85%** of observed walk-ins within two minutes and close **≥90%** of observed completed cuts on the tablet. Convert **≥2 of 3** trial shops at **1,000 EGP/month**. Compare the service log with independent shift observations. |
| **Public launch · months 4–6** | Open standard onboarding in both neighborhoods and aim for **two new paid shops per month** as a planning assumption. Introduce deposit-backed slots shop by shop only after the owner agrees how to make reservations visible and preserve walk-in fairness, and a concierge deposit test supports rollout. | Reach **6–8 active paying shops**. In shops using deposits, achieve **≥50%** paid deposits among eligible booking attempts, **<5%** no-shows among confirmed bookings and **zero** observed double bookings. Track paying shops lost and the opening paying-shop count each month against the Milestone 1 **<1.3% monthly churn** benchmark. |
| **Expansion · months 7–12** | Build shop density in New Cairo and Nasr City, launch cross-shop discovery and test a **10–15%** commission on new-customer bookings attributable to the marketplace. | Reach **20–24 active paying shops**, **over 300 commission-eligible marketplace bookings per month** and **35–40%** repeat active customers at 60 days in tracked cohorts before expanding to a new area. |

Three trials set a fixed denominator within Milestone 1’s two-to-three-shop beta range. Milestone 1 also proposed a separate concierge experiment with **30–50 EGP** deposits and a **50%** payment gate. Schedule that experiment with consenting shops before automated booking rollout. The prototype reservation task in section 2.3 provides evidence about the interaction. The concierge experiment measures whether customers actually pay.

The public-launch target needs **4–6 net paying shops** on top of two beta conversions. Two new paid shops per month would add six before churn, provided trials start early enough to finish in the launch window. Moving from **6–8** to **20–24** active paying shops over six months requires **12–18 net additions**, or **2–3 per month**. The upper end requires higher acquisition throughput, with enough gross additions to replace churn. These are planning targets.
