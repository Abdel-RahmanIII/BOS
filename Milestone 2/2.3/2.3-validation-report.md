# 2.3 Validation experiment

**BarberQueue Egypt · 22 November 2026**

## Hypothesis and success criteria

Milestone 1 assumption #3 is that customers can check a preferred barber's live wait and reserve a haircut easily, and front-desk staff can add walk-ins quickly. We expected at least **8 of 10 customers** to check Omar's wait and reserve a Haircut with him for any available time today, correctly and unaided within **60 seconds**. We expected **3 of 3 receptionists** to add a Haircut walk-in to Omar's queue, correctly and unaided within **15 seconds**. Both groups had to meet their targets. [Milestone 1 riskiest assumptions, #3](../../MIlestone%201/Milestone1.md#3-customers-can-check-live-queue-and-book-easily-and-receptionists-can-add-walk-ins-quickly) · [Pre-test protocol, 21 November 2026](Evidence/2026-11-21-usability-protocol.md)

## Setup

The study moderator ran the tests in person with customers and receptionists using a timed, clickable prototype. The prototype used simulated barbers and queues. The app timed the interval from **Start** to final submission. It logged the selected barber and service, and for customers it captured the wait-view event for the selected barber and the reservation slot. The protocol called for one first attempt per eligible participant and a moderator to record assistance, hesitation, interruptions and the customer's recall of the wait time.

The locked study contains 14 completed in-person records from 22 November 2026. The 11 customer sessions used English. Two receptionist sessions used Egyptian Arabic and one used English. All participants used the moderator's laptop. The moderator read the task aloud and confirmed that all participants attempted it without assistance. One staff participant worked as both a barber and a receptionist and regularly handled walk-ins at his own shop, meeting the staff eligibility rule.

The plan called for 10 customers. The moderator invited an additional participant who was present with a friend taking the test. The results include all 11 customer attempts. The original target was 8 of 10.

## Actual results

Participants completed 14 unaided attempts during the in-person tests. Ten of 11 customers completed the task correctly within 60 seconds, and two of three receptionists completed it correctly within 15 seconds. The [locked Study results dashboard](Evidence/locked-results-names-hidden.png) documents task accuracy and timing. The moderator confirmed assistance status separately.

## Findings

- **Customer flow.** One customer submitted a haircut for Karim after 6.5 seconds (024c2dcf). Each customer record contains a wait-view event for the barber ultimately selected. Understanding of the wait estimate remains unverified.
- **Receptionist flow.** All three submissions selected Omar and are marked correct on the dashboard. Two finished in 8.7 and 10.9 seconds. The third finished in **15.1 seconds** (bbc16b6f), exceeding the 15-second limit. Step timings and hesitation observations were unavailable.
- **Scope.** These results cover two prototype tasks on a laptop. Usability on customer phones and front-desk devices, live queue accuracy, staff adoption in a rush, deposit behavior and willingness to pay require separate evidence.

## Decision

**Revise and retest before front-desk rollout.** The customer group met its threshold, supporting retention of the wait-and-reserve flow. The receptionist group missed its threshold because one otherwise correct attempt exceeded 15 seconds. The experiment required both groups to meet their targets.

Put **Add walk-in** beside each barber in the staff queue so entry can open with that barber selected. Make Haircut, Beard trim and Haircut and beard visually distinct in both languages and show the barber and service together before submission on both flows. Keep the selected barber prominent from the customer’s wait screen through reservation. Before retesting, verify that the displayed choices, saved events and automatic score agree for all services and both languages.

The CEO and product owner should lead a fresh timed retest before the live pilot. The CTO can revise the screens and verify scoring. The COO can recruit eligible customers and regular walk-in handlers. Use the same thresholds with 10 new customers and 3 new receptionists, confirm role eligibility and record device, assistance, hesitation, wait recall and any deviation by session ID. Document the reason for any extra attempt.
