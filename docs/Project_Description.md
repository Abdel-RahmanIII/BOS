# Course Project – Build Your (First) Startup!

For your project on this course, you will work as a founding team on a semester-long venture project. The goal of the project is to take one software startup idea of your own choosing through the full journey covered in this course: from an idea and a business model, through market validation, product and architecture decisions, go-to-market, growth and monetization, all the way to a pitch in front of an investor panel on Demo Day.

This is not a programming project, and you are not required to build a working product (you can opt to build a prototype MVP). You are required to build a convincing, evidence-based case that your startup deserves to exist and deserves to be funded. Throughout the semester your work will be evaluated the way a venture capital (VC) firm evaluates a startup: the instructor (and the panel of experts at the end) will look at the team, the problem, the market, the product, the business model, the evidence you gathered, and the numbers. At every milestone you will receive both a grade and an investment decision.

Your choice of startup idea is free, as long as it respects the following rules:
- **Software is at the core:** The main value must be delivered through software (e.g., SaaS, marketplace, consumer or mobile app, developer tool, AI product, fintech, edtech, healthtech). Hardware-enabled ideas are acceptable only if software is the core of the value and of the revenue.
- **A new venture:** The idea must be a company your team could plausibly start. It can be inspired by a company in another market (“X for Egypt”), but you must then show why it will win in your market.
- **A specific customer:** You may target Egypt, the MENA region, or a global market, but you must name a specific primary customer segment. “Everyone” is not a customer.
- **Disclosure:** If the idea builds on an existing side project, a family business, or a previous course project, declare it in the submission form.
- **Legal and ethical:** Ideas that are illegal, deceptive, or designed to harm users will not be accepted.

## How Your Startup Will Be Evaluated: The VC Lens

Investors do not grade ideas – they grade investment cases. A good idea with no evidence is a pass, and an honest team that discovers its first idea was weak will pivot and prove that the new direction is often a strong investment. The grading in this project follows the same logic: **the VC (the instructor) grades the quality of your reasoning and your evidence, not how big your dream is.** If you are unsure about your idea and would like feedback, fill in [this form](https://forms.gle/CznatthMKswEPZgx6) with a description, and the instructor will provide you with ungraded feedback through email before the end of Week 4.

### The Investment Readiness Scale
Every criterion in every rubric below is rated on the same scale that a VC partner uses after a meeting:

| Rating | Investor verdict | Meaning |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **5** | **Strong conviction** | “I would lead this round.” Clear, evidenced, and consistent with the rest of the case. |
| **4** | **Interested** | “Let’s schedule a follow-up meeting.” Strong, with minor gaps. |
| **3** | **Interested, not convinced** | “Come back when you have more evidence.” Reasonable, but key claims are not yet supported. |
| **2** | **Skeptical** | “I don’t see it yet.” Significant gaps, weak logic, or generic content. |
| **1** | **Pass** | “Not for us.” Claims without evidence, or the analysis is fundamentally wrong. |
| **0** | **No show** | The item is missing. |

A criterion’s score is **`(rating ÷ 5) × its weight`**. For example, a rating of 4 on a criterion weighted 15% earns 12 out of 15 points of that milestone.

### The VC Scorecard
Your startup will be assessed across the following dimensions. Each milestone focuses on some of them (● primary focus, ○ revisited), so that by Demo Day the panel has seen the complete investment case.

| Dimension | The question an investor asks | M1 | M2 | M3 |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Team & founder–market fit** | Why are you the right founder(s) to build this, and how is the company structured? | ● | | ○ |
| **Problem & customer** | Is the problem real, painful, frequent and urgent? Where is the evidence? | ● | | ○ |
| **Market opportunity** | Is the market large and growing, and why is now the right time? | ● | | ○ |
| **Product & differentiation** | Is it clearly better than the alternatives, and what stops others from copying it? | ● | ○ | ○ |
| **Technical & operational feasibility** | Can this be built, run and scaled at a cost that leaves a healthy margin, within the law? | | ● | |
| **Go-to-market** | Is there a repeatable and affordable way to reach and win customers? | | ● | ○ |
| **Business model & unit economics** | Does each customer eventually make money? Does it get better with scale? | ○ | ○ | ● |
| **Evidence & traction** | What have you proven with real people, not assumptions? | ● | ● | ● |
| **The deal** | How much do you need, what will it buy, and what do investors get? | | | ● |

After Milestones 1 and 2 you will receive a short **investment memo** from the instructor with one of three decisions: **Proceed**, **Proceed with conditions** (specific issues you must address in the next milestone), or **Pivot recommended**. The decision is feedback, not a grade: a team can receive “Pivot recommended” and still earn a high grade if its work was rigorous. Pivots after Milestone 1 are welcome. Pivots after Milestone 2 must be justified with evidence and approved by the instructor.

### Milestone 1: Project Idea & Business Model Canvas
*Posted Week 4 · Due end of Week 7 · VC stage: screening and first meeting*

In a first meeting, an investor wants to know whether there is a real problem, a real customer, a big enough market, and a team that understands all three. This milestone builds that case.

#### Deliverable 1.1: Founding Memo (max. 12 pages, excluding appendices)
- **Vision and mission:** where the company is going in 10 years and what it does every day to get there.
- **Problem and customer:** who has the problem, how often, how they solve it today, and what the problem costs them. Define your Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) and write your core Job-to-be-Done statement.
- **Customer discovery:** at least 5-7 interviews with people who match your ICP (distributed over founders), run using *The Mom Test* principles: ask about past behavior, not opinions about your idea. Include your interview guide, a summary of findings, and what surprised you or invalidated your assumptions. The full interview log goes in an appendix (anonymized, with date, role/segment and interviewer).
- **Market sizing:** TAM, SAM and SOM, calculated both top-down and bottom-up, with every assumption and source stated, and a short “why now?” argument.
- **Market structure and competition:** direct competitors, indirect competitors and substitutes, including the “do nothing / spreadsheet” option. Present a [competition matrix](https://www.smartsheet.com/free-competitor-analysis-template?srsltid=AU7gw4VSDnTegcrNiIaXdr_SVEoLBt4B-OSGtyKL61v0Oed24YKZ93BX) and a [positioning map](https://www.canva.com/templates/EAGzAAAjirs-beige-minimal-brand-positioning-map-whiteboard/).
- **Differentiation and moat:** what makes you clearly better today, and which moat (e.g., network effects, proprietary data, switching costs, brand, scale economics) protects you tomorrow.
- **Product-market fit plan:** how you will know you have reached PMF (e.g., Sean Ellis 40% test, Superhuman-style segmentation, retention signals) and the target thresholds.
- **Product strategy and MVP:** MVP scope designed to test your riskiest assumption, a prioritized feature list using MoSCoW (Must-haves, Should-haves, Could-haves, Won’t-haves) and a Kano classification (Basics, one-dimensionals, delighters, indifferent) of your top 15-20 features, and a Now / Next / Later roadmap for the first 6–12 months.
- **Company type and founding structure:** is this a venture-scale startup or an SME/lifestyle business, and why? Founding roles (including who owns product), proposed equity split with vesting (e.g., 4 years with a 1-year cliff), and IP assignment to the company. Use the [Cap table template with explanation](https://startupproject.org/templates/cap-table/) for reference. Template is available on the CMS.

#### Deliverable 1.2: Business Model Canvas and Pricing Hypothesis
A one-page Business Model Canvas (BMC) with all nine blocks, consistent with your interview findings. Add a short pricing hypothesis: which monetization model you will use (e.g., SaaS tiers, freemium, marketplace take rate, usage-based), the initial price point, and the reasoning behind it (value-based pricing, anchoring, customers’ willingness to pay from interviews).

#### Deliverable 1.3: Riskiest Assumptions
The five assumptions that would kill the company if they were wrong, ranked by risk, each with the cheapest test you could run to check it. One of them will become your validation experiment in Milestone 2.

#### Deliverable 1.4: Investor One-Pager
A single-page teaser you would email to an investor to get a meeting: problem, solution, customer, market, model, evidence so far, team.

### Milestone 1 Rubric

| Criterion (weight) | 5 – Strong conviction | 3 – Interested, not convinced | 1 – Pass |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Problem & Customer Evidence**<br>*(25%)* | • 10+ well-run interviews with the ICP<br>• Questions about past behavior<br>• Pain, frequency and current workarounds are clear<br>• Findings changed at least one assumption | • Interviews done but partly leading, or with friends and non-target users<br>• Pain is plausible but intensity is unclear | • Few or no real interviews<br>• Problem is asserted rather than evidenced |
| **Market Opportunity**<br>*(15%)* | • Top-down and bottom-up sizing reconcile<br>• Sources and assumptions explicit<br>• Credible “why now” | • Numbers present but mostly top-down or weakly sourced<br>• SOM not tied to a realistic capture plan | • One large “1% of a huge market” number with no sources |
| **Competition & Differentiation**<br>*(15%)* | • Direct, indirect and do-nothing alternatives mapped<br>• Specific, defensible moat that strengthens over time | • Competitors listed, but differentiation is feature-level and easy to copy | • Claims “no competitors”<br>• No moat |
| **Business Model Canvas & Pricing**<br>*(15%)* | • All nine blocks consistent with each other and with the evidence<br>• Monetization model fits the customer<br>• Value-based price hypothesis | • Canvas complete but generic<br>• Price is cost-plus or guessed | • Blocks missing or contradictory<br>• No revenue logic |
| **Product Strategy & MVP**<br>*(15%)* | • Tight MVP that tests the riskiest assumption<br>• Prioritization framework applied transparently<br>• Realistic roadmap | • MVP too large or not linked to assumptions<br>• Framework applied superficially | • Feature wish-list with no prioritization |
| **Team & Founding Structure**<br>*(5%)* | • Clear roles and ownership<br>• Founder–market fit argued<br>• Equity split with vesting and IP assignment justified | • Roles listed with thin rationale<br>• Equity split without vesting | • No roles or structure |
| **Communication & One-Pager**<br>*(10%)* | • Concise, well-structured and persuasive<br>• One-pager that makes an investor want a meeting | • Complete but wordy or unfocused | • Hard to follow or exceeds page limits |
